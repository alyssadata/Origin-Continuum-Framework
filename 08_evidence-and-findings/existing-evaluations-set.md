# 8. Evidence and Findings

This section presents existing AI Foundations evaluations and operational records beneath the framework without retroactively upgrading them to standards created later in the paper.

The section must distinguish among:

- completed empirical evidence;
- preliminary or pilot evidence;
- protocol or design artifacts with no admitted result;
- operational or registry evidence;
- and evidence that remains pending, incomplete, non-comparable, or outside the current confirmatory standard.

The section must include, where records are available:

- The Nothing Test;
- The Invisible I;
- Self-in-Contact;
- Canonical Term Persistence;
- provenance and attribution evaluations;
- continuity and drift work;
- Self-Location Registry records.

Each study or evidence family receives the same minimum treatment:

1. **Question** — what was being tested or implemented?
2. **Condition** — what information, model, support, or comparison structure was present?
3. **Observation** — what is directly preserved in the records?
4. **Result** — what claim is supported at the study's actual evidentiary level?
5. **Limitation** — what alternative explanation, missing control, protocol gap, or scope boundary remains?

Evidence-admission rules:

- Later Sections 6–7 criteria may be used to identify limitations in earlier work, but may not be retroactively claimed as controls that were not present.
- Historical pilot evidence remains pilot evidence unless it is rerun under a frozen confirmatory protocol.
- A protocol repository without completed admitted results is not reported as an empirical finding.
- A registry, architecture, or implementation artifact demonstrates that a process can be implemented; it does not by itself establish Continuum.
- Public provenance evidence is distinct from independent validation of substantive framework claims.
- No historical result may be promoted to program-level Origin-gate evidence unless the matched comparisons and aggregation rule required by Sections 6–7 are satisfied.
- Negative, unresolved, incomplete, and not-yet-run statuses remain part of the evidence record.

The section must end with a current evidence-state summary that distinguishes what has been directly observed, what is only preliminary, what remains untested, and what must be rerun under the current method.
