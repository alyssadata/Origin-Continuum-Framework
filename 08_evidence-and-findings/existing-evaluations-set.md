# 8. Evidence and Findings

This section presents the prior AI Foundations research record relevant to **Origin | Continuum** without retroactively upgrading earlier work to standards created later in the paper.

The section must distinguish both **admission status** and **evidentiary function**.

Admission status includes:

- completed empirical evidence;
- preliminary or pilot evidence;
- protocol or design artifacts with no admitted result;
- operational or registry evidence;
- and evidence that remains pending, incomplete, non-comparable, or outside the current confirmatory standard.

Evidentiary function includes:

- **empirical** — preserved observations bearing directly on a tested behavioral question;
- **methodological lineage** — dated protocols, hypotheses, definitions, or scoring structures showing how the research question was operationalized before this anchor framework;
- **operational implementation** — artifacts showing that a source, registry, memory, continuity-support, or governance process can be implemented without thereby establishing the central selfhood claim.

The section must include, where records are available:

- The Nothing Test;
- The Invisible I;
- Self-in-Contact;
- Canonical Term Persistence;
- provenance and attribution evaluations;
- continuity and drift work;
- Self-Location Registry records.

Each study or evidence family receives the same minimum treatment:

1. **Question** — what was being tested or implemented?
2. **Condition** — what information, model, support, or comparison structure was present?
3. **Observation** — what is directly preserved in the records?
4. **Result** — what claim is supported at the study's actual evidentiary level?
5. **Limitation** — what alternative explanation, missing control, protocol gap, or scope boundary remains?

Evidence-admission rules:

- Admission is governed first by the study's own controlling status record, frozen protocol, results ledger, or equivalent canonical record where one exists.
- A result file does not override a controlling ledger that marks the study not run, incomplete, non-comparable, or otherwise unadmitted.
- If no controlling status record exists, the artifact may be admitted only at the narrowest status directly supported by preserved records; uncertainty is resolved downward rather than upward.
- Later Sections 6–7 criteria may identify limitations in earlier work, but may not be retroactively claimed as controls that were not present.
- Historical pilot evidence remains pilot evidence unless it is rerun under a frozen confirmatory protocol.
- A protocol repository without completed admitted results is methodological lineage, not an empirical finding.
- A registry, architecture, or implementation artifact demonstrates that a process can be implemented; it does not by itself establish Continuum.
- Public provenance evidence is distinct from independent validation of substantive framework claims.
- No historical result may be promoted to program-level Origin-gate evidence unless it was produced within the matched comparison structure and aggregation rule required by Sections 6–7.
- A contextualized-versus-unsupplied-context contrast is not an Origin manipulation unless Origin is the prospectively isolated comparison factor.
- Negative, unresolved, incomplete, contaminated, and not-yet-run statuses remain part of the evidence record.
- Inclusion and exclusion decisions must identify the controlling record or preserved basis on which the status was assigned.

The empirical summary must preserve the size and status of the underlying evidence. Pilot findings may not be restated as broad general conclusions without identifying their limited run base.

Methodological lineage and operational implementation must be summarized separately from empirical findings.

The section must end with a current evidence-state summary that distinguishes what has been directly observed, what is only preliminary, what is methodological or operational rather than empirical, what remains untested, and what must be rerun under the current method.
