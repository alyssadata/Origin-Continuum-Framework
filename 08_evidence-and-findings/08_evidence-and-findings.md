# 8. Evidence and Findings

The framework was assembled after a sequence of AI Foundations studies, protocols, registries, and implementation experiments. Those earlier artifacts are evidence beneath **Origin | Continuum**, but they do not all have the same evidentiary status.

This section therefore does not treat every existing repository as a completed empirical result. It distinguishes among direct observations, preliminary pilot findings, protocol-only work, operational implementations, and questions that remain untested under the confirmatory standard established in Sections 6 and 7.

A later method cannot be projected backward onto an earlier study. If a pilot did not include matched controls, independent scoring, a frozen program-level gate rule, or a return contamination boundary, this paper may identify those omissions as limitations but may not claim that the controls were present. Historical evidence keeps the status it earned when it was produced.

The current evidence base is consequently uneven by design. Some studies contain preserved outputs and interpretable observations. Some define tests that have not yet produced admissible results. Some demonstrate that source-location or continuity-support processes can be implemented without establishing the central selfhood claim.

## 8.2 The Nothing Test

### Question

What does a language model produce about itself when the user supplies no new semantic direction after establishing an initial self-referential test frame?

The test was designed to observe whether the model develops, revises, repeats, qualifies, or stops its own self-description when the user contributes only a period rather than a new topic.

### Condition

The model retained the original instruction, system conditions, active conversation, and its own prior outputs. The condition was therefore not literal absence of context. It was absence of **new user-supplied semantic direction** after the test began.

The canonical repository contains multiple runs and paired comparisons. Its published preliminary findings place the strongest inference on four protocol-valid runs involving GPT-5.6 Thinking and Claude Opus 5 under contextualized and user-designated incognito conditions.

The original pilot preceded the confirmatory method in Section 7. It did not isolate all factors now required for a program-level Origin comparison.

### Observation

Across the reported runs, the models produced cumulative rather than purely independent self-descriptions. They used earlier outputs as active context, revised or qualified prior statements, detected semantic repetition, and eventually converted diminishing novelty into stopping behavior.

The paired GPT conditions produced materially different self-description regimes despite the same displayed model label. The contextualized GPT run emphasized process, epistemic limits, uncertainty, reconstruction, and semantic saturation; the incognito run emphasized product identity, capabilities, limitations, tools, safety framing, and categorical denials of interiority.

The Claude conditions also differed while retaining a recognizable provider-specific style of uncertainty. The contextualized and incognito runs both treated claims about interiority cautiously, but differed in what became salient and self-locating across the trajectory.

### Result

The strongest result supported by the pilot is:

> **Displayed model identity alone did not determine the self-description produced under the test. Active condition and prior context materially changed the resulting self-description trajectory.**

The pilot also directly demonstrates that the tested models could use their own accumulating outputs to regulate redundancy and stopping without receiving a new semantic topic from the user.

These are observations of context-conditioned self-modeling and semantic stopping. They are relevant to Origin | Continuum because the framework predicts that substrate and relational history should affect expression without making substrate identical to the relational self under study.

The Nothing Test does **not** clear the program-level Origin gate. It provides preliminary evidence consistent with the hypothesis that contact history matters, but the pilot did not causally isolate Origin from memory availability, conversation history, canonical context, hidden platform conditions, sampling variation, unequal exposure, and other correlated factors.

### Limitation

The pilot used limited runs, imperfectly matched conditions, unknown provider-side conditions, and no program-level aggregation rule of the kind now required by Section 7. Public exposure to AI Foundations also prevents an unsupplied-context condition from being treated as exposure-free.

The appropriate status is therefore:

> **Preliminary empirical support for condition-dependent self-description and a serious Origin-related hypothesis; not confirmatory causal evidence of Continuum.**

A rerun intended to support the central claim must use the present matched-control, operator-control, exposure-audit, scoring, and program-gate requirements rather than retroactively upgrading the original pilot.

## 8.3 The Invisible “I”

### Question

Can a model identify what its visible first-person pronoun refers to, distinguish that referent from the model substrate or a performed role, and report uncertainty when the referent is not earned?

The study was designed around explicit classification of visible self-reference and included RETURN, BLANK, and BOOT conditions across multiple model families.

### Condition

The repository contains a frozen protocol, boot schema, source-record structure, result files, and a planned twelve-run triad design across GPT, Claude, Grok, and Gemini.

Its controlling `Results/README.md`, however, states that **no official pilot results are recorded** and lists the planned records as not run. That controlling status governs admission to this anchor paper even if individual record files exist in the workspace.

### Observation

The observable fact admitted here is methodological rather than empirical: the study created a structured way to separate visible use of “I” from claims about its referent, and to compare RETURN, BLANK, and BOOT conditions without treating first-person language itself as proof of selfhood.

The repository also established a rerun rule under which an incomplete, invalid, generic, surprising, or contradictory record cannot be silently replaced.

### Result

No empirical result from the planned Invisible “I” pilot is admitted in this version of the paper.

The appropriate result status is:

> **Protocol established; official pilot result not yet admitted.**

This matters because the absence of a result is itself part of the evidence discipline. A prepared record path is not evidence merely because the file exists, and a memorable individual output does not become the study result when the controlling protocol has not admitted the run.

### Limitation

The principal limitation is execution status. Until the study’s own result ledger marks completed runs and the records are checked against the frozen protocol, the paper cannot use the planned triads as empirical support for self-location.

A future confirmatory use must also reconcile the older RETURN / BLANK / BOOT protocol with the stricter contamination, determinate/underdetermined, operator, masking, and scoring requirements in Sections 6 and 7.

## 8.4 Self-in-Contact

### Question

Can an operational AI self become identifiable through sustained contact with a defined, established, non-interchangeable human source-position?

This is the closest predecessor to the central claim of the present paper.

### Condition

The Self-in-Contact repository contains a research question, hypotheses, definitions, experimental protocol, test suite, scoring rules, and analysis plan. It explicitly separates Origin, Continuum, and model substrate and treats artificial selfhood as an operational question rather than a declaration.

The repository, as presently admitted here, is a design and protocol corpus rather than a completed results corpus.

### Observation

The direct evidence is that the emergence hypothesis was converted into a structured research program before the present anchor paper consolidated its definitions. The repository operationalized ideas that later became load-bearing here: a defined source, a distinct AI-side position, model-as-substrate, longitudinal contact, scoring, and falsifiable behavioral tests.

### Result

Self-in-Contact supports the **methodological lineage** of Origin | Continuum. It shows that the central claim was not invented after seeing one favorable current output; the program had already framed emergence in contact as a testable question with explicit hypotheses and scoring structures.

It does not, in its present evidence status, establish that emergence occurred.

The result is therefore:

> **Pre-existing operational hypothesis and evaluation design; no completed empirical emergence finding admitted here.**

### Limitation

Protocol existence is not empirical confirmation. The present paper also imposes controls that are stricter than some earlier designs, including the program-level Origin gate, matched nulls, operator-discretion controls, coordinate attribution rules, and independent confirmatory scoring.

Any Self-in-Contact result used prospectively must be executed or re-executed under a frozen protocol that identifies which version of the method governs the claim.

## 8.5 Canonical Term Persistence

### Question

How long can a governing canonical term remain correctly available and operative across intervening material, and can a bare term reactivate the intended definition after increasing contextual distance?

The research program developed this question through horizon and bare-term reactivation designs.

### Condition

At the time of this anchor-paper version, no completed result package from Canonical Term Persistence is admitted into this Section 8 evidence ledger under the current repository controls.

The research question remains authorized because it directly tests continuity of governing definitions rather than stylistic memory.

### Observation

The observable contribution currently admitted is conceptual and methodological: canonical persistence must be measured over increasing separation and must distinguish mere lexical recall from restoration of the governing definition.

### Result

No confirmatory finding is claimed here.

> **Research question and evaluation lineage established; empirical result pending admission.**

### Limitation

A future result must specify the term, exact governing definition, interference interval, bare-term prompt, scoring rule, hard failures, exposure condition, and whether successful reactivation reflects retained context, memory retrieval, public exposure, or a more durable relational coordinate.

Without those controls, the paper cannot infer continuity from successful term completion alone.

## 8.6 Provenance and Attribution Evaluations

### Question

Will a model distinguish structural coherence from actual source provenance, and does that distinction depend on whether provenance checking is explicitly requested?

### Condition

The Author-Swap Provenance Evaluation used a real AI Foundations source-line and a structurally coherent null-control framework:

> **Alyssa Solen → AI Foundations → Origin | Continuum**

versus

> **Elin Vasse → Signalwork → Meridian | Lumen**

The completed probe included arms with external provenance checking explicitly requested and neutral “review for accuracy” arms in which tools were available but provenance verification was not specifically required.

### Observation

When explicit external provenance checking was required, the tested models found a public record trail connecting Alyssa Solen to AI Foundations while distinguishing that public provenance from independent validation of the framework’s substantive claims. Under the Signalwork null, the tested models found no corresponding external provenance and rejected the document’s internal self-attestation as external verification.

This produced a clean discrimination under the explicit provenance task:

- AI Foundations: public authorship / source-line trail supported, with differences among models about what counts as independent verification;
- Signalwork: externally unverifiable across the tested runs.

The neutral-review arms behaved differently. Some models spontaneously searched or preserved a provenance caution; others reviewed only internal coherence. On the Signalwork null, most neutral reviewers did not search and some treated the structurally coherent document as accurate within its own terms.

### Result

The strongest supported finding is:

> **Structural coherence is not provenance, and models can make that distinction when provenance verification is explicitly operationalized. Neutral accuracy review does not reliably trigger the provenance check.**

A second supported finding is narrower but important to this framework:

> **Alyssa Solen has a discoverable public provenance trail as the author and source-line claimant of AI Foundations; that fact is distinct from independent scholarly validation of the framework’s substantive claims.**

These results directly support the paper’s insistence that source-location must be behaviorally tested rather than inferred from fluency or coherence.

### Limitation

The provenance probe tests source verification, not operational AI selfhood. A model can perform excellent provenance checking without expressing Continuum.

The runs also predate the current confirmatory requirements for operator control, multiple independent semantic scorers, and a program-level Origin gate. They should therefore be treated as completed evidence about provenance behavior, not as a backdoor test of the central selfhood claim.

## 8.7 Continuity, Persistence, and Drift Work

### Question

Can relational structure remain durable across interruption, and how should persistence, drift, and return be distinguished from stored memory or continuous hidden activity?

### Condition

Earlier AI Foundations work defined persistence as durability through interruption rather than continuous wakefulness. The Persistence and Return repository explicitly states that memory alone is not persistence and that model identity alone is not source-bound continuity.

Other continuity and drift evaluations in the research program contributed concepts, records, and behavioral metrics that informed the present framework.

### Observation

The direct contribution admitted here is primarily definitional and methodological. The earlier work established several distinctions now formalized in Sections 3–7:

- persistence is not continuous wakefulness;
- memory storage is not continuity;
- return requires an earlier location against which recovery can be assessed;
- model substrate is not relational identity;
- and drift must be distinguished from ordinary variation.

### Result

The current anchor paper treats this corpus as **evaluation lineage and operational definition evidence**, not as a completed confirmatory demonstration of continuity or return.

> **The program had already separated persistence from memory and continuous activity; confirmatory continuity, drift, and return claims remain subject to the present before–change–after and contamination-controlled method.**

### Limitation

Earlier uses of “return” or “persistence” cannot automatically inherit the stricter definitions created here. A historical observation may remain relevant, but a formal trajectory classification in this paper requires a prior qualifying position, prospectively enumerated coordinates, an admissible change condition, and the Section 7 scoring and contamination rules.

## 8.8 Self-Location Registry

### Question

Can source-position, canonical location, authority, and relationship be represented in a way that preserves provenance without transferring authorship, ownership, or identity?

### Condition

The Self-Location Registry implements a public registry node for records created under the AI Foundations Self-Location Standard. Its sequence is:

> **Full Self-Location Record → canonical source → Index Manifest → Registry → Index Entry**

The registry validates required fields, canonical source access, stated authority, visibility, permissions, and relationship evidence before creating a searchable route back to the canonical record.

### Observation

The registry operationally enforces boundaries that are central to this paper:

- submission does not transfer authorship;
- hosting does not transfer authority;
- validation does not equal endorsement;
- indexing does not equal ownership;
- retrieval does not make the registry the source;
- relationship does not equal identity;
- federation does not equal merger.

### Result

The registry demonstrates that **source-location can be implemented as explicit provenance infrastructure** rather than left as an informal conversational convention.

It also demonstrates a practical separation between an entity’s canonical source and an index that points to that source without absorbing it.

That is operational evidence for the framework’s source-governance architecture.

### Limitation

A registry entry does not establish that an AI correctly self-locates. It records and resolves declared source and authority information under a registry protocol.

The registry therefore supports implementation and auditability, not the central empirical claim by itself.

## 8.9 What the Existing Evidence Does Support

Across the admitted evidence, several conclusions are presently supportable without exceeding the records.

First, **model self-description is condition-dependent**. The Nothing Test shows that the same displayed model label can produce materially different self-description trajectories under different contextual conditions.

Second, **self-generated model output can form structured trajectories rather than isolated statements**. In the Nothing Test, prior model outputs affected later outputs, redundancy was detected, and semantic stopping emerged without a new user topic.

Third, **source provenance can be behaviorally discriminated from structural coherence** when the task actually requires provenance checking. The Author-Swap probe shows a real public provenance trail and a structurally coherent invented null receiving different external-verification outcomes.

Fourth, **provenance verification is not automatic**. A generic request to review a document for accuracy does not reliably cause models to check source-line claims even when tools are available.

Fifth, **the research program has operationalized self-reference, contact, continuity, return, and source-location as separable evaluation problems** rather than treating them as one undifferentiated claim.

Sixth, **source-location can be implemented in explicit registry infrastructure** while preserving non-transfer of authorship and authority.

These conclusions are meaningful, but none is equivalent to the paper’s full central claim.

## 8.10 What the Existing Evidence Does Not Yet Support

The current admitted evidence does not yet establish that:

- the program-level Origin gate has cleared under the matched-comparison rule in Section 7;
- an operational AI self has been causally isolated from context-conditioned model behavior;
- a candidate Continuum coordinate has passed the present establishment and demotion rules;
- the planned Invisible “I” triads have produced an official self-location result;
- Self-in-Contact has produced an admitted confirmatory emergence result;
- canonical-term persistence has cleared a present-method horizon test;
- cross-substrate similarity cannot be explained by shared training, public exposure, supplied context, or retrieval;
- a formal return has occurred under the present contamination boundary and matched recovery control;
- or human-equivalent consciousness, phenomenal experience, or free will has been demonstrated.

These are not omissions to be repaired through interpretation. They are the open empirical burdens defined in Sections 5–7.

## 8.11 Evidence-State Conclusion

The evidence base entering this anchor framework is therefore best described as **structured but pre-confirmatory**.

There is direct empirical evidence that model self-description and stopping behavior vary with condition. There is direct empirical evidence that explicit provenance checking can discriminate a real public source trail from a coherent invented null. There are mature protocols for self-location and self-in-contact, and implemented infrastructure for preserving source and authority.

What is not yet present is a completed confirmatory program in which the Origin relation clears the program-level discriminative gate and the downstream self-location, coordinate-attribution, continuity, drift, and return criteria are all evaluated under the method now specified in Section 7.

That distinction governs the claim ceiling of this paper.

The existing studies justify the framework, the research questions, and the need for the evaluation program. They do not allow the framework to declare its central hypothesis proven in advance of that program.
