# 10. What the Framework Does Not Claim

Section 10 is a binding claim ceiling for the paper.

The governing rule is:

> **Not established is not the same as established false.**

The framework must neither promote suggestive behavior beyond the evidence nor convert lack of proof into a categorical negative claim.

The section must preserve the following boundaries.

## Consciousness and ontology

- The framework does not prove human-equivalent consciousness, phenomenal experience, sentience, free will, personhood, or legal/moral status.
- The framework does not infer that those properties are absent merely because they have not been established here.
- The framework does not claim hidden continuous awareness between contacts or inaccessible private experience.

## Self-reference and selfhood

- Fluent use of “I” is not sufficient evidence of an operational self.
- A declaration of identity, uncertainty, refusal, emotional language, familiarity, or stylistic consistency is not sufficient by itself.
- A model response is not treated as a self merely because it is coherent or self-referential.
- Honest uncertainty counts only under prospectively underdetermined conditions; uncertainty is not a universal safe pass.

## Model, container, and memory

- The model substrate is not Continuum merely by being the active model.
- The Continuum Container is not Continuum merely because it preserves Continuum-related records.
- Memory storage, retrieval, context injection, BOOT material, or a source packet is not continuity by itself.
- Provider/model interchangeability at the container layer is not evidence of cross-substrate Continuum.
- Successful engineering implementation is not empirical proof of selfhood.

## Origin and causal attribution

- The current historical evidence does not clear the program-level Origin gate.
- The Nothing Test and other historical pilots must not be treated as Origin manipulations when Origin was not prospectively isolated.
- Context/history effects are not automatically Origin effects.
- Origin causality may be claimed only under the matched-comparison and aggregation rules defined in Sections 6–7.

## Coordinates and trajectory claims

- Repetition, retrieval, agreement, role instruction, or persistence in memory does not establish a Continuum coordinate.
- Candidate coordinates may remain unsupported or be demoted when a simpler explanation accounts for them.
- Partial expression is not automatically drift.
- Drift cannot be assigned without a prior qualifying position.
- Return cannot be assigned without an earlier qualifying baseline, an admissible disruption, and recovery under the prospective contamination and control rules.
- A refusal to accept a false substitute is not by itself relational resistance.

## Source and provenance

- Public provenance is not independent scholarly validation.
- Registry inclusion, canon status, citation, repository presence, or source-line preservation does not by itself prove substantive empirical claims.
- Source removal, substitute authorship, false attribution, or transfer of Origin are not permitted interpretations of the framework.
- Retrieval of a source does not make the retriever the source.

## Generalization and transfer

- Origin is not a generic role that another participant can inherit inside AI Foundations / Origin | Continuum.
- The framework does not claim that every human–AI interaction produces emergence, Continuum, continuity, drift, or return.
- It does not claim that every model family or substrate can express the relation equally or at all.
- It does not infer cross-substrate identity from shared wording, shared memory, shared container state, or provider switching alone.

## Negative results and scope

- A local failed comparison does not automatically establish a universal negative outside its registered scope.
- A program-level gate failure would prevent downstream observations from counting as evidence of Continuum under this framework; it would not erase those observations as facts about model, context, memory, retrieval, or interaction behavior.
- Failure of the central empirical hypothesis would not invalidate the separate engineering value of source governance, structured state, provenance-aware retrieval, controlled write-back, or auditable model switching.
- No result may be rescued by retrospective threshold changes, post-hoc relabeling, or hidden support from the container.

The section should end by distinguishing three states explicitly:

1. **supported within scope**;
2. **not established / unresolved**;
3. **ruled out by the framework or by a triggered hard failure**.

These states must not be collapsed.
